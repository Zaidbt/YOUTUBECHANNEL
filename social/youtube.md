## What does LSD actually do to your brain? (out/lsd.mp4)
Title: What LSD actually does to your brain 🧠🤯 #shorts
Description:
A dose is about 100 micrograms (0.0001 g, roughly 1/5000 of an aspirin pill), yet it lasts 8 to 12 hours. Normally serotonin taps its 5-HT2A receptor for an instant and the neuron fires once. LSD has the same two-ring core, so it fits the same lock, and then a lid on the receptor (extracellular loop 2, seen in a 2017 crystal structure) snaps shut over it. ## How does a light bulb make light? (out/bulb.mp4)
Title: How a light bulb ACTUALLY makes light 💡🤯 #shorts
Description:
A 10 W LED fires about 10 quintillion photons every second. Inside its 1 mm chip sit two crystals: one with extra electrons (N), one with "holes", missing electrons (P). Switch on 3 volts and they're pushed to the edge, where an electron falls into a hole and its extra energy flies out as one photon. The bigger the fall (the energy gap), the bluer the light: blue was so hard to make it won the 2014 Nobel Prize. White LEDs are a blue chip under a yellow phosphor coat. Old bulbs heated a wire to ~2,500°C and turned ~90% of the power into heat; LEDs give the same light with 75% less power and last up to 25x longer. Sound on 🔊 every flash of light is a note.

## How does anesthesia switch you off? (out/anesthesia.mp4)
Title: How anesthesia ACTUALLY switches your brain off 💉😵 #shorts
Description:
Propofol, the milky drug nicknamed "milk of amnesia", knocks you out in about 30 seconds. Awake, your neurons fire nonstop above their firing threshold. GABA is the brain's brake: it opens a door in the GABA-A receptor that lets chloride in, calming the neuron. Propofol jams that door wide open, chloride floods in, and the neuron sinks below threshold into silence. Across billions of neurons, the EEG turns from fast chatter into huge slow waves, and the thalamus stops relaying signals to the cortex. It isn't sleep: a nudge wakes you from sleep, surgery doesn't wake you from this. It's closer to a reversible coma. Stop the drip, the drug washes out, and you're awake in minutes. Sound on 🔊 the music goes under with you.

## What is the CRACK when you crack your knuckles? (out/knuckles.mp4)
Title: That "crack" isn't your bones. Here's what it really is 🫧 #shorts
Description:
When you crack your knuckles, nothing breaks. Between the two bones of every knuckle is a sealed capsule full of slippery joint fluid with gas dissolved in it. Pull the finger and the capsule stretches, the pressure inside drops, and the gas suddenly rips out of the fluid into a bubble. That's the crack: real-time MRI in 2015 caught the sound happening exactly as the bubble is born. The gas needs around 20 minutes to dissolve again, which is why you can't crack the same knuckle twice in a row. And arthritis? One doctor cracked only his left hand for 60 years (about 36,500 times): no arthritis in either hand, and it won him an Ig Nobel Prize in 2009. Try it: crack one, then try the same one again 😏

## How a 2-stroke engine works (out/twostroke.mp4)
Title: Why 2-stroke engines sound like THAT 🏍️💥 #shorts
Description:
Half the parts, twice the bangs. A 2-stroke engine has no valves: just 3 holes in the cylinder wall. Going up, the piston squeezes the fuel on top and sucks fresh fuel into the crankcase underneath. Spark, bang, the piston slams down, and on the way down it uncovers the holes: burnt gas out the exhaust, fresh fuel up the side channel. The fat exhaust pipe (an expansion chamber) even bounces a pressure wave back to plug the exit. Result: one bang every turn, where a car engine gets one every two. The catch: oil is mixed into the fuel, which is that blue smoke. At 10,000 rpm that's 167 bangs a second. Sound on 🔊 every bang is a drum hit.

## What happens inside a manual gearbox (out/gearbox.mp4)
Title: What ACTUALLY happens when you shift gear ⚙️🏁 #shorts
Description:
Engine → clutch → gearbox → wheels. Inside the gearbox, every gear is always meshed and always spinning, but loose on its shaft. When you move the stick, a fork slides a collar that locks ONE gear to the shaft. Before it locks, a friction cone (the synchro) matches their speeds, so nothing grinds. 1st gear: a small gear drives a big one, about 3.5x the push but slow. Top gear: big drives small, cruise speed. The clutch unplugs the engine while you switch. That grinding sound? The synchro lost the race. Sound on 🔊 every shift is a hit, and the beat changes key with every upshift.

## How your phone vibrates (out/vibration.mp4)
Title: What's spinning inside your phone right now? 📳 #shorts
Description:
When your phone buzzes, something inside is spinning about 11,760 times a minute. Older and cheaper phones have a tiny motor (an ERM) with a half-moon of metal stuck on its shaft. Because the weight is off-center, every turn it yanks the motor (and the whole phone) around in a little circle, about 196 times a second. The chip switches the motor on and off to make patterns: bzz... bzz... Newer phones (iPhone Taptic Engine, most flagships) don't spin anything: a magnet sits between two springs and a coil pushes it back and forth at its natural rhythm (a linear resonant actuator). It starts and stops in a few milliseconds, so it can "tap" instead of buzz. Sound on 🔊 the buzz is the lead synth: 196 Hz is a G, and every time the video speeds up, the note goes up an octave.

## How Face ID works (out/faceid.mp4)
Title: Your phone fires 30,000 invisible dots at your face 👁️ #shorts
Description:
Every time you unlock your phone, it scans your face in the dark. First a flood illuminator lights you up with infrared light, which you can't see, so it works at night. Then a dot projector fires more than 30,000 infrared dots at your face. An infrared camera reads how each dot lands: dots on your nose are closer than dots near your ears, so together they make a 3D depth map. The chip turns that map into maths and compares it with your saved map, which stays locked inside the Secure Enclave and never leaves the phone. A printed photo can't fool it: it's flat, so every dot lands at the same depth. Odds that a random stranger's face unlocks your phone: about 1 in 1,000,000 (Touch ID was 1 in 50,000). ## How a lock gets picked (out/lockpick.mp4)
Title: Your front door opens without the key. Here's how 🔓 #shorts
Description:
Inside most front door locks are 5 little pin stacks: a key pin, a driver pin and a spring. The driver pins cross the gap between the turning part (the plug) and the body, the "shear line", so the plug can't turn. The right key lifts every gap exactly to that line. Picking does it without the key: a tension wrench twists the plug very slightly, and because no lock is machined perfectly, ONE pin jams first. Lift it with a pick until it clicks: the driver pin stays up on the edge of the plug, and the next pin jams. Repeat 5 times and it turns. With 5 pins and up to 10 heights, that's up to 100,000 different keys, beaten by feel. Better locks use spool pins that fake the click. Sound on 🔊 every pin that sets is a note.
