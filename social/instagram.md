Turn the sound on: every tick you hear is a tick you see.

## Post · How do bees build perfect hexagons? (out/bees-pixel.mp4)
How do bees build perfect hexagons? 🐝🎧
No plan. No tools. No boss.

Young bees hang in chains and make wax from 8 glands under their belly. They chew it soft and press it into walls, cell by cell.

Why hexagons? Circles leave gaps. Squares use more wall. Hexagons leave no gaps AND use the least wax (~7% less than squares) — and wax costs bees about 8 kg of honey per kg.
Mathematicians only proved it in 1999. Bees knew all along.

## Post · How does fusion power the Sun? (out/fusion.mp4)
How does fusion power the Sun? ☀️🎧
The Sun isn't on fire. Its core is 15 million °C, so hot that atoms fall apart into bare protons.

Protons repel each other… but it's so hot and crowded they slam together and fuse: deuterium, then helium-3, then helium-4. 4 protons in, 1 helium out, and the helium is 0.7% lighter.
That missing mass becomes energy. E = mc².

☀️ 600 million tonnes of hydrogen fused every second
☀️ 4 million tonnes turned into pure energy every second
☀️ that light takes thousands of years to get out… and 8 minutes to reach you

## Post · How does your DNA copy itself? (out/dna.mp4)
How does your DNA copy itself? 🧬🎧
Every cell holds 2 meters of DNA, packed into a nucleus ~6 µm wide. It's a code with just 4 letters: A always pairs with T, C with G.

To copy it, a helicase unzips the helix, and a polymerase grabs free letters and matches every single one. Each copy ends up with 1 old strand + 1 new strand, and it even proofreads itself: about 1 mistake per billion letters.

🧬 ~50 letters per second
🧬 at thousands of forks at once
🧬 ~330 billion new cells in your body every day, every one copied like this

## Post · How do screens work? (out/screens.mp4)
How does your screen make every color? 📱🎧
Zoom in far enough and it's all pixels. Each pixel is 3 tiny lights: red, green and blue.
Red + green = yellow. Green + blue = cyan. All three = white.
Each light has 256 brightness levels → 16.7 million colors, and your phone has over 2 million pixels, redrawn 60–120 times a second. Your eye blends it all into one picture.

Sound on 🔊 the pixel IS the drum machine: red = kick, green = snare, blue = hats.

## Post · How do noise-cancelling headphones work? (out/anc.mp4)
How do noise-cancelling headphones work? 🎧✈️
Noise is a wave. A tiny mic on your headphones hears it, a chip flips it upside down, and the speaker plays that "anti-noise" with your music. Peak + valley = silence.

🔇 recalculated thousands of times a second
✈️ best on low hums: engines, trains, air conditioning
🗣️ voices and clicks partly slip through (the ear pads help)

## Post · How does a chameleon change color? (out/chameleon.mp4)
How does a chameleon change color? 🦎🎧
It's not just paint. Under its skin sit layers of cells packed with tiny crystals that reflect light.
Calm: the crystals sit close together and bounce back blue. Blue + the yellow pigment above it = green.
Excited: the cell stretches, the crystals spread apart and reflect yellow, orange, red.

🦎 it changes in seconds, by moving crystals, not by mixing paint
🦎 mostly to talk (to rivals and mates), not to hide
🦎 bonus: its tongue shoots out up to 2× its body length

## Post · How does a light bulb make light? (out/bulb.mp4)
How does a bulb turn electricity into light? 💡🤯

✨ a 10 W LED fires ~10 quintillion photons a second
🧱 inside its 1 mm chip: 2 crystals, one with extra electrons (N), one with holes (P)
⚡ 3 volts push them to the edge
⬇️ an electron falls into a hole → its extra energy flies out as 1 photon
🌈 bigger fall (energy gap) = bluer light: red → green → blue
🏆 blue was so hard to make it won the 2014 Nobel Prize
🟡 white LED = blue chip + a yellow phosphor coat
🔥 old bulbs: a wire at ~2,500°C, ~90% of the power wasted as heat
💚 LEDs: same light, 75% less power, last up to 25x longer

Sound on 🔊 every flash of light is a note.

## Post · How does anesthesia switch you off? (out/anesthesia.mp4)
This milky drug switches you off in ~30 seconds. 💉😵

🥛 propofol, nicknamed "milk of amnesia"
⚡ awake, your neurons fire nonstop above their firing threshold
🚪 GABA is the brake: it opens a chloride door (the GABA-A receptor)
🔓 propofol jams that door wide open → chloride floods in
🤫 the neuron sinks below threshold → silence
🌊 EEG: fast chatter → huge slow waves
📵 the thalamus stops relaying signals to the cortex → no awareness
😶 not sleep: a nudge wakes you from sleep, surgery doesn't wake you from this. It's closer to a reversible coma.
☀️ stop the drip → it washes out → awake in minutes

## Post · What is the CRACK when you crack your knuckles? (out/knuckles.mp4)
That "crack" isn't your bones. 🫧

🦴 between the two bones of each knuckle: a sealed bag of slippery joint fluid, with gas dissolved in it
🖐️ pull the finger → the bag stretches → the pressure drops
💥 the gas suddenly rips out of the fluid into a bubble: CRACK (seen live on MRI in 2015)
⏳ the gas needs ~20 minutes to dissolve again → that's why you can't crack the same knuckle twice
🏆 arthritis? One doctor cracked only his LEFT hand for 60 years (~36,500 cracks). Neither hand got arthritis. Ig Nobel Prize, 2009.

## Post · How a 2-stroke engine works (out/twostroke.mp4)
Half the parts. Twice the bangs. 🏍️💥

🕳️ no valves: just 3 holes in the cylinder wall
⬆️ going up, the piston squeezes the fuel on top + sucks fresh fuel into the crankcase below
💥 spark → bang → the piston slams down
🔄 going down it opens the holes: smoke out, fresh fuel in
〰️ the fat pipe bounces a pressure wave back to plug the exit
1️⃣ result: 1 bang every turn (a car engine: 1 every 2)
💨 the catch: oil is mixed into the fuel → that blue smoke
🔥 at 10,000 rpm: 167 bangs a second
